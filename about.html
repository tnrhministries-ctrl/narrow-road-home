I didn't choose this. God did.

The story behind The Narrow Road Home.

A couple of years ago, I started a YouTube channel because I felt God calling me to it — not because I had it figured out, and not because I believed in myself enough to think anyone would watch.

Honestly, that part hasn't gotten easier. I still struggle to believe in myself. I look at other creators doing this so much better, reaching so many more people, and it's easy to wonder if any of this actually matters — if 25 views or a short with a thousand views is even worth the time it takes to make.

But I've learned this isn't about the numbers I can see. It's about obedience to a God who sees everything I can't.

One of our interviews — with a missionary raising support for his work in Japan — had 140 views when I first checked. Not a big number by any measure. But somewhere on the other side of the world, someone saw it. And that person reached out and helped fund his ministry. I never saw that coming. I couldn't have engineered it. That's not something I did — that's what happens when you show up and do the small, obedient thing, and let God handle the rest.

That's what The Narrow Road Home is built on. Real, raw conversations — about faith, doubt, addiction, purpose, and the fight to keep believing when it's hard. Not polished answers. Not performance. Just honesty, pointed back to Jesus, for whoever needs to hear it right now.

You were born onto a battlefield. There's a real enemy who wants you discouraged, isolated, and silent — and I believe with everything in me that God created you to keep fighting anyway. I'm not writing that as someone who has it all figured out. I'm writing it as someone who needs to hear it as much as you might.

If you're walking through something right now — doubt, addiction, grief, weariness — I hope something on this site meets you exactly where you are. That's the whole point of it.

A small number, a real difference

140 views. One missionary. One person who saw it, reached out, and helped fund his work in Japan. I don't always get to see the fruit of this — but I know it's there.

Wherever you're at, you're welcome here.
